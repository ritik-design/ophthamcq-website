## What does FAICO stand for?

FAICO means Fellow of the All India Collegium of Ophthalmology. AIOS describes it as a fellowship in an ophthalmic subspecialty for members who meet its qualification and experience rules. It is awarded through an assessment process, so it should not be confused with the clinical fellowship or senior-residency training used to establish eligibility.

The official AIOS member-benefits page uses this expansion. Older and third-party pages sometimes write "Fellowship of" instead of "Fellow of." For an examination answer or formal biography, use the wording from AIOS.

FAICO is also different from FICO. FICO refers to the International Council of Ophthalmology examination pathway. The similar abbreviations make this an easy search and viva trap, but the organisations, eligibility rules and assessment routes differ.

<!-- section_id: faico_eligibility -->
## Who is eligible for FAICO 2026?

AIOS requires current membership, a government-recognised postgraduate qualification in ophthalmology and at least two years of experience after post-graduation. Candidates must also meet one listed subspecialty route through fellowship training, senior residency or practical work. AIOS sets 30 September 2026 as the cut-off for the relevant experience requirements.

### The core requirements

The live AIOS page lists:

1. **AIOS membership.**
2. **A recognised basic postgraduate ophthalmology qualification:** DO, DNB, MD, MS or a recognised international qualification.
3. **At least two years of experience after completing post-graduation.** AIOS says fellowship or senior-residency time can be included.

### The additional subspecialty route

A candidate must also meet one of these routes:

- completed a subspecialty fellowship, or at least 18 months of that fellowship;
- completed senior residency, or at least 18 months of senior residency; or
- at least three years of practical work experience in the selected subspecialty.

Read the wording as a document check, not as a puzzle to solve informally. If your dates overlap, a certificate is pending or your qualification is unusual, ask AIOS to confirm eligibility in writing.

## Which FAICO subspecialties are available?

AIOS lists ten FAICO awards for the current cycle: glaucoma, retina and vitreous, cornea, comprehensive ophthalmology, refractive surgery, uvea, cataract or phaco, paediatric ophthalmology and strabismus, oculoplastic surgery, and neuro-ophthalmology. A member may apply for one specialty at a time and may complete no more than two specialties.

The ten listed tracks are:

1. Glaucoma
2. Retina and Vitreous
3. Cornea
4. Comprehensive Ophthalmology
5. Refractive Surgery
6. Uvea
7. Cataract or Phaco
8. Paediatric Ophthalmology and Strabismus
9. Oculoplastic Surgery
10. Neuro-ophthalmology

Choose the track that matches your documented training and daily clinical depth. FAICO is a subspecialty assessment. Broad reading across all ophthalmology is less useful than being able to defend decisions within the field named on your application.

## What FAICO exam pattern is confirmed for 2026?

AIOS confirms an online MCQ test followed by specialty-specific OSCE and viva assessment. Its live 2026 page does not state the number of MCQs, examination duration, negative-marking rule, written pass mark or OSCE cut-off. Candidates should use current instructions and their admit card for those operational details rather than an older newsletter.

The distinction matters because a 2018 AIOS newsletter described 60 MCQs in one hour, a 70% written pass mark, no negative marking and a 50% OSCE cut-off. Those figures document the 2018 exam. They are not evidence that the 2026 rules are identical.

### Stage 1: online MCQ test

The current official page identifies the format but not the blueprint. A sensible preparation model is therefore based on subspecialty decision-making rather than a guessed question count:

- mechanisms and classifications;
- diagnosis and close differentials;
- investigation and image interpretation;
- indications, contraindications and complications;
- management choices by disease stage;
- major evidence or guidelines that change practice.

### Stage 2: OSCE

AIOS states that OSCE centres differ by specialty. The live page does not publish a station list. Prepare a repeatable response order for any image, instrument, investigation or clinical task:

1. identify the task or finding;
2. state the features that support it;
3. give the likely diagnosis or interpretation;
4. name the closest relevant differential;
5. state the next investigation or management principle;
6. add the complication that changes urgency or approach.

This six-step frame is OphthaMCQ preparation advice, not an AIOS rule.

### Stage 3: viva

A viva rewards prioritised reasoning. Start with the diagnosis or decision. Add the evidence, alternatives and important complication. A long recital creates more places for the examiner to probe. Short does not mean shallow; each sentence should carry a defensible clinical point.

## How should candidates prepare for 30 August?

With 13 days remaining at the 17 August fact-check, the useful job is retrieval, not collecting another textbook. Run a timed baseline, sort errors by cause, revise high-impact decision points and repeat mixed blocks. Keep OSCE and viva practice alive in short sessions so the second stage does not begin from zero.

### Pass 1: classify errors

Take a timed mixed set from your selected subspecialty. Put every error into one of four buckets:

- missing knowledge;
- misread stem;
- two-option confusion;
- time or confidence error.

Only the first bucket automatically needs more reading. The others require better discrimination, slower stem parsing or timed practice.

### Pass 2: revise decisions

Build one-page sheets around the places where answers split:

- diagnostic criteria and grading;
- investigation of choice and characteristic image;
- first management principle and escalation trigger;
- procedure selection;
- intraoperative and postoperative complications;
- trial or guideline that changes the decision.

### Pass 3: retrieve under time

Use mixed timed blocks. Review every option, not only the keyed answer. In a subspecialty paper, distractors are often true statements attached to the wrong stage, image, anatomy or patient context.

## A specialty-first revision framework

### Retina and Vitreous

Prioritise OCT and angiography interpretation, diabetic retinopathy, vascular disease, macular disorders, retinal detachment, surgical principles, tamponade choice and complications. Use the [retina revision hub](/topics/retina) for connected review.

### Glaucoma

Link disc, OCT and field findings to stage and treatment. Rehearse target pressure, medical escalation, laser, trabeculectomy, tubes and MIGS as decisions rather than separate lists.

### Cornea and Refractive Surgery

Work through microbial keratitis, dystrophies, ectasia, ocular surface disease, keratoplasty choice, topography or tomography, refractive eligibility and postoperative complications.

### Cataract or Phaco

Organise around biometry, IOL selection, machine parameters, difficult-cataract planning, capsular complications, anterior vitrectomy and postoperative problem-solving.

### Paediatrics and Strabismus

Combine visual development and amblyopia with measurement, incomitance, paralytic patterns, surgical planning and paediatric red flags.

### Uvea, Oculoplastics, Neuro-ophthalmology and Comprehensive Ophthalmology

Use the same chain for each case: localisation, likely diagnosis, decisive evidence, next step and complication. That keeps an image-based question and a viva response inside one reasoning system.

<!-- section_id: faico_osce_viva_prep -->
## What should candidates do after the written test?

Do not wait for the September result to speak your answers aloud for the first time. Keep a small daily OSCE and viva block after the written paper. Practise image descriptions, instrument recognition, investigation interpretation and one-minute management answers, while checking AIOS for the centre and specialty instructions that apply to your track.

A practical weekly loop is:

1. two image or investigation stations;
2. two procedure or instrument stations;
3. one complication-management station;
4. five rapid viva questions recorded on a phone;
5. one review of answers that became vague under follow-up.

The recording is useful because it exposes answers that start clearly and then dissolve into an unranked list.

## Common FAICO preparation mistakes

- Treating 2018 pattern details as confirmed 2026 rules.
- Reading every subspecialty instead of going deep in the selected track.
- Memorising procedures without indications, alternatives and complications.
- Practising MCQs without an error log.
- Ignoring images and investigations.
- Waiting for the written result before beginning OSCE and viva work.
- Giving a precise number in viva when the number cannot be defended.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is FAICO the same as a clinical fellowship?

No. A clinical fellowship or senior residency is a period of training. FAICO is an assessment-based award for an eligible AIOS member in a selected subspecialty. Training and experience form part of eligibility, but passing the FAICO process is a separate event.

### Can I apply immediately after MS or DNB?

The degree alone does not meet the published requirements. AIOS also requires at least two years of post-PG experience plus one of the listed fellowship, senior-residency or practical subspecialty experience routes. Ask AIOS to review unusual timelines.

### How many FAICO specialties can I take?

AIOS says a member may apply for one specialty at a time and may complete a maximum of two specialties.

### Is the 2026 application still open?

No. AIOS lists 31 May 2026 as the deadline. This statement was checked on 17 August 2026.

### How many questions are in the 2026 written test?

The current AIOS page confirms an online MCQ test but does not state the 2026 question count. The 60-question figure belongs to a 2018 AIOS newsletter and should not be presented as the current rule without newer official instructions.

### Is FAICO worth doing?

That depends on your training stage, specialty practice and professional goals. AIOS presents FAICO as a subspecialty fellowship benefit for eligible members. Claims that it guarantees jobs, promotion, referrals or a specific career outcome are not supported by the published exam page.
