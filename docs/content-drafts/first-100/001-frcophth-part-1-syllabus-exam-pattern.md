The FRCOphth Part 1 syllabus is not a list to memorise from a coaching handout. The Royal College of Ophthalmologists (RCOphth) assesses the learning outcomes for years 1 and 2 of the Ophthalmic Specialist Training curriculum. For the current examination format, RCOphth states that Part 1 consists of two MCQ papers on one day, with 90 questions and two hours for each paper.

That gives you a useful starting point, but not a revision plan. The sensible approach is to turn the learning-outcome boundary into a working matrix: identify the foundation domains, map each to a reliable reference, test yourself in mixed blocks, and keep an error log that records *why* an option was wrong. Dates, fees, booking windows and remote-proctoring instructions change by sitting, so confirm them in the [RCOphth examinations calendar](https://www.rcophth.ac.uk/examinations/) before acting on any timetable in this article.

> **2026 rule check:** This guide was checked against RCOphth’s Part 1 page on 18 August 2026. Where an official calendar, information pack, application form or your candidate instructions differs, the newer official document wins.

## Part 1 at a glance

| Question | What the current RCOphth page says | What you should do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Who can sit? | Doctors with a medical qualification approved by the GMC or Irish Medical Council; prior ophthalmology experience is not required. | Check your own qualification and application route early, especially if applying from overseas. |
| How many attempts? | Up to six attempts; attempts before August 2013 do not count towards that limit. | Treat each sitting as a planned attempt, not a diagnostic mock. |
| Paper format | Two MCQ papers. | Build stamina for two separate timed papers rather than one long revision session. |
| Questions and timing | 90 questions in each paper; two hours for each paper. | Practise a steady pace, then preserve time to review marked items. |
| Same-day schedule | Both papers take place on the same day, with a one-hour break. | Rehearse food, break and concentration management in a full mock. |
| Marking | The pass mark is set in advance using the Ebel method and may vary by sitting. | Do not plan around a guessed percentage or an old pass mark. |
| Fees and dates | Fees are reviewed annually; dates are published in the exams calendar. | Use the official page, not an archived blog post, before budgeting or booking travel. |

These are operational facts, not a prediction of what will appear on your paper. The assessment blueprint tells you how to prepare your method; it does not justify an invented claim that a particular chapter is worth a fixed number of marks.

## What “the FRCOphth Part 1 syllabus” actually means

RCOphth describes Part 1 as an assessment against the learning outcomes for years 1 and 2 of the OST curriculum. In practice, that means your preparation must handle foundational science and its clinical use. A fact recalled without context is fragile. So is a clinical answer that has no grasp of optics, anatomy, pathology or pharmacology underneath it.

Many third-party pages offer percentage distributions by topic. They can be useful prompts for revision, but they are not the College’s current published guarantee of question weightage. Use them cautiously. Your durable source hierarchy should be:

1. the current RCOphth Part 1 page, calendar and information pack for rules;
2. the curriculum learning outcomes for the scope of learning;
3. standard ophthalmology references for the science;
4. your own timed-question error log for where you lose decisions.

That hierarchy prevents two common mistakes: treating an old recall sheet as the syllabus, and treating one commercial question bank as a substitute for a reference text.

## The revision domains you need to cover

The following is a practical revision map, not an official RCOphth percentage table. It is built to help a candidate organise the foundational material that recurs across Part 1-style questions.

### Anatomy, embryology and neuroanatomy

You need spatial rather than purely verbal knowledge. Be able to move from orbit to apex, cornea to endothelium, retina to visual pathway, and EOM action to a pattern of movement. Draw pathways from memory. Then label the lesion, blood supply, relation or developmental derivative that turns a broad stem into a single best answer.

Useful self-tests include:

- localising an optic-nerve or chiasmal lesion from a field description;
- relating a cranial-nerve lesion to ocular motility findings;
- identifying an anatomical relation relevant to orbital surgery or regional block;
- recalling tissue origin, layer order or drainage route without a diagram in front of you.

If you can only recognise the answer when you see a labelled image, the knowledge is not ready for a timed paper. Cover the labels, redraw it, then explain the clinically relevant consequence in one sentence.

### Physiology, biochemistry and cell biology

This domain is where mechanistic stems often become difficult. Do not revise aqueous dynamics, phototransduction, accommodation, tear film, corneal deturgescence, retinal physiology and ocular blood flow as isolated paragraphs. For each mechanism, write four fields: normal sequence, control point, failure state and measurable consequence.

For example, an IOP question may test production, outflow, pressure relationships or a drug mechanism. The task is to discriminate the tested step, not to recite every fact about glaucoma. A one-line mechanism card followed by mixed questions is usually more useful than repeatedly highlighting a chapter.

### Optics and refraction

Optics rewards deliberate practice because the same principles recur in different clothing. Build a formula sheet, but do not stop there. For each formula or diagram, ask what happens when one variable changes and what a patient, instrument or retinal image would show. Practise sign convention and units until they are automatic.

Your core set should include vergence, thin-lens relationships, principal points, image magnification, ametropia, accommodation, prisms, retinoscopy and optical instruments. Elkington is commonly used by candidates because it makes the diagrams explicit; whichever text you choose, work questions immediately after the section rather than leaving all calculation practice for the last month.

For a product preview that may fit a structured revision system, see [FRCOphth Step 1 MCQs](/products/frcophth-step-1-mcqs). It is optional preparation material, not an official RCOphth resource or a replacement for the current syllabus.

### Pathology, microbiology, immunology and pharmacology

These subjects become manageable when you revise them through patterns of injury and response. Instead of keeping a long drug list, group medicines by receptor or target, ocular use, adverse effect and contraindication. Instead of memorising organisms as a flat list, pair each with host setting, tissue pattern, investigation clue and important differential.

For pathology, make comparison cards. One side is the histological process or named lesion; the other is the mechanism, morphology and clinical association that distinguishes it from its nearest distractor. The same method works for hypersensitivity, wound healing, inherited disease and neoplasia.

### Epidemiology, statistics and evidence interpretation

These questions are often neglected because they feel separate from ophthalmology. They are also highly trainable. You should be able to calculate or interpret sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, likelihood ratios, risk measures, confidence intervals, study designs, bias and basic trial terminology.

Do not memorise a definition without using it. Take a diagnostic-test table and calculate the measure. Read an abstract and identify the population, comparator, endpoint and most important limitation. Under exam pressure, this turns an apparently unfamiliar question into a repeatable sequence.

### Clinical integration

Part 1 is foundational, but the most useful revision does not artificially separate basic science from clinical meaning. A question about ocular motility may require anatomy and physiology. A corneal question may require microbiology, pharmacology and tissue response. A retinal-image question may require optics before it requires a named diagnosis.

At the end of every topic, add one column to your notes: “What would this look like in a stem?” It forces you to convert passive knowledge into a decision.

## Build a syllabus-to-revision matrix

The matrix below is a better starting point than a colour-coded list of chapters. Make it in a spreadsheet or notebook and update it after each timed block.

| Learning area | Specific decision | Primary reference | Error type | Next retrieval task | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Optics | Determine image location after a lens change | Elkington chapter and worked examples | Formula chosen correctly; sign convention wrong | Solve five unseen calculations aloud | +1 day, +7 days |
| Neuroanatomy | Localise a field defect | Anatomy source + pathway sketch | Confused tract and radiation | Redraw pathway from memory, then answer three mixed stems | +1 day, +7 days |
| Pharmacology | Identify mechanism and adverse effect | Core pharmacology text | Knew class; missed contraindication | Make a two-column comparison card | +3 days, +14 days |
| Statistics | Interpret a screening-test result | Statistics notes + original table | Used prevalence incorrectly | Recalculate with new numbers | +1 day, +7 days |

The useful part is the error type. “Got optics wrong” is not a revision instruction. “Used the correct equation but reversed the sign convention” is. Over several weeks, your matrix reveals whether you need more content, more discrimination between close options, or more timed practice.

## A six-month preparation template for doctors who are working

This is a planning template, not a promise about what any candidate needs to pass. Adjust the volume to your baseline, rota and official sitting date.

### Months 1 and 2: map the foundations

Take a short diagnostic mixed block before choosing resources. Do not use the score as a prediction. Use it to identify unfamiliar domains and recurring error types. Work through the major foundation areas in parallel: one science-heavy session, one calculation/diagram session and one mixed retrieval session each week works better than completing one specialty before touching the next.

During this phase, build the matrix and make concise retrieval cards. Avoid rewriting textbooks. If a note does not help you answer a question, redraw a mechanism or discriminate between two options, it is probably too long.

### Months 3 and 4: shift from chapters to mixed decisions

Start timed mixed blocks. Review every wrong answer, every guessed correct answer and every question narrowed to two options. For each, record the discriminating feature. A question bank is most valuable when it reveals an assumption you did not know you were making.

Use the [FRCOphth preparation guide](/exams/frcophth) for the site’s broader route map and [free FRCOphth sample questions](/free-mcqs/frcophth) for short retrieval sessions. Keep the official College documents separate from any commercial resource: they answer different questions.

### Month 5: practise the two-paper day

Once weekly, simulate the attention demands of two papers rather than doing only a single block. Use the official current timing as your limit. Preserve the one-hour break in the simulation. Notice whether errors increase from fatigue, rushing, hunger, calculation slips or second-guessing; each needs a different response.

Do not review only the score. Review the last 20 questions in each paper separately. If accuracy drops late, practise pacing and flagging. If it drops after a certain domain, revisit the conceptual gaps in your matrix.

### Month 6: consolidate, do not expand endlessly

At this stage, your strongest material is your error log, formula sheet, pathway sketches and comparison cards. Continue mixed practice, but stop adding large new resources because they create the illusion of coverage without retrieval. Use the current RCOphth calendar and candidate instructions to confirm booking, system checks and examination-day requirements.

## How to use questions without memorising recalls

Candidate recalls can point to a topic, but they are not a reliable reproduction of a paper. Wording, options and context may be incomplete. Label them as recalls, verify the underlying concept in a standard source, and do not build an entire plan around a presumed repeat.

After each block, classify the question before reading the explanation:

1. **Knowledge gap:** you did not know the fact or mechanism.
2. **Discrimination error:** you knew both options but missed the feature that separated them.
3. **Process error:** calculation, unit, negation or reading mistake.
4. **Timing error:** you knew it but committed too quickly or too late.

The corrective action should match the category. Knowledge gaps need a source and retrieval card. Discrimination errors need a comparison table. Process errors need a checklist. Timing errors need timed exposure. This is much more useful than doing another random set of questions immediately.

## Common Part 1 planning errors

- **Using an old date or fee page.** RCOphth says fees are reviewed annually and directs candidates to its calendar for dates. Verify the current sitting.
- **Treating unofficial weightage as a blueprint.** It can guide attention, but it is not a College guarantee.
- **Deferring optics.** Calculations and diagrams improve through spaced practice, not a final-week formula binge.
- **Reading only within subspecialty silos.** Mixed questions expose the basic-science connection that a chapter-by-chapter plan hides.
- **Counting questions instead of decisions.** One carefully reviewed error can change several future answers; fifty unreviewed questions rarely do.

## Pre-application and study checklist

Before you lock in a revision date, confirm your eligibility and attempt history, read the current Part 1 page, save the application closing date, check the current fee and read the online-proctoring instructions. If you hold an overseas qualification and are applying for the first time, RCOphth says you must use the first-time form and upload an attested qualification certificate through that process.

Then choose a realistic weekly volume, create the revision matrix and start a mixed error log. The goal is not to imitate somebody else’s timetable. It is to make every study session produce a decision you can retrieve under timed conditions.
