There is no honest, permanently complete list of ophthalmology fellowships in India. Posts open and close by institution, department, intake and subspecialty; duration, eligibility, stipend, registration requirements and application route can change with a new notice. A list that gives those facts without a dated primary source is worse than incomplete—it can send you to the wrong application route.

The practical alternative is a **current-list method**: decide the clinical route you want, identify institutions you would genuinely join, and keep only opportunities supported by the recruiting institution’s current page or notice. This is career planning for doctors after MS/DNB, not a guarantee of selection or a substitute for an institution’s written terms.

## First decide what you are choosing

“Fellowship” can describe very different jobs. Before opening recruitment pages, choose the decision you are actually making.

| Route | The decision to make before applying | Evidence worth seeking |
|---|---|---|
| Retina | Medical retina, vitreoretinal surgery, imaging-heavy service, or a mixed role? | clinic/theatre split, imaging exposure, emergency role, supervision |
| Cornea and refractive | Surface/cornea, keratoplasty, refractive work, or anterior segment? | procedure observation versus supervised operating, graft/eye-bank exposure |
| Glaucoma | Medical glaucoma, imaging/field interpretation, lasers or surgery mix? | longitudinal clinic responsibility, perimetry/OCT teaching, surgical role |
| Paediatric ophthalmology / strabismus | Paediatric service, squint, ROP exposure, or a mixed post? | age mix, screening/ROP responsibilities, orthoptic support, theatre role |
| Oculoplasty / orbit | Lids, lacrimal, orbit, oncology interface, trauma, or cosmetic exposure? | emergency roster, theatre allocation, multidisciplinary access |
| Uveitis / neuro-ophthalmology / ocular oncology | Diagnostic, imaging and multidisciplinary depth | clinic volume, imaging access, case conference and research support |
| Comprehensive / cataract / community ophthalmology | High-volume systems work, cataract, outreach or broad independent-practice preparation | audited outcome feedback, supervision, outreach role and training record |

This table is not a claim that any named institution offers every route. It is the question set you use to read an actual post. A familiar hospital name does not tell you whether the current fellowship is primarily observership, service, research, supervised operating, or a combination.

## What a reliable fellowship listing looks like

Keep an opportunity in your spreadsheet only after it has a source, a date and a contact route. Your minimum record should contain:

| Field | What “verified” means |
|---|---|
| Institution and department | A current official recruitment, education or departmental page—not an aggregator repost |
| Fellowship title and subspecialty | Exact wording from the notice |
| Intake and deadline | Explicit date or rolling-application wording, plus the day you checked it |
| Eligibility | Exact degree, registration, experience and document requirements stated by the institution |
| Duration and employment terms | Written notice, HR document or offer; `unknown` until documented |
| Application route | Official portal, official email, form or named contact |
| Role and supervision | Curriculum/notice detail, then questions put to the programme |
| Evidence saved | URL plus PDF or dated screenshot, because notices disappear |

Do not infer a stipend from an older cohort, a social post or another fellowship in the same hospital. Likewise, a general “education” page does not prove that a particular subspecialty intake is open today.

## Where to look: a primary-source search ladder

Start with the institutions, not a national aggregator. This produces a smaller list at first, but every surviving entry is usable.

### 1. Institutional education and careers pages

Use the recruiting institution’s own site as the authority for its fellowship. For example, [Aravind Eye Care System’s ophthalmologist-fellowship page](https://aravind.org/fellowships/) and [LVPEI’s education-course record for a long-term ophthalmology fellowship](https://www.lvpei.org/services/education/courses/long-term-fellowship-2-faculty-in-ophthalmology/) demonstrate why an institution-first search is more reliable than a copied directory: the programme title, page structure and application details live with the provider and can be updated there.

These examples are **routes to verify**, not an endorsement, a ranking, or a statement that any currently visible page represents every programme, intake or eligibility condition. Read the page on the day you apply and follow the contact details it supplies.

### 2. Department pages, hospital notices and PDF advertisements

Some posts appear first in a department notice or PDF rather than a permanent fellowship index. Search the official domain for the subspecialty plus “fellowship”, “vacancy”, “career”, “education” and the relevant year. Save the notice, because a search result can persist after the intake closes.

### 3. Professional-society and meeting notices—lead, then verify

The [All India Ophthalmological Society](https://osindia.org/) is useful for professional context, meetings and notices. Use a society listing as a lead only. Return to the institution’s own notice before you spend time on an application, because neither a society website nor an alumni post can establish the current terms of an employer.

### 4. Registration and document checks

Your registration status and documents are part of the route, not paperwork for the last week. The [National Medical Commission](https://www.nmc.org.in/) is the public regulatory starting point for current registration information. It is not a fellowship-placement service, and it does not remove the need to read a hospital’s own eligibility wording.

## Build the shortlist in 90 minutes

This is a practical first pass for residents who are on call and cannot spend days browsing.

1. Write your first and second subspecialty choice. Add one non-negotiable, such as a particular city, supervised theatre exposure, research time or a community-service route.
2. Make a list of 15 institutions across realistic locations. Include places you would actually accept; a famous programme that you cannot join adds noise.
3. Search each official domain, then add only current or dated primary-source findings to the spreadsheet.
4. Mark every unknown as `unknown`; never fill a blank with a remembered stipend, duration or “usual” eligibility.
5. Rank the remaining posts with three columns: **fit**, **evidence quality**, and **application urgency**. A well-documented opportunity you want is more actionable than a vague listing with a prestigious name.
6. Recheck the top five before applying. A date visible on an old PDF is not current simply because the PDF remains online.

The point is not to create a giant list. It is to reach a defensible shortlist before deadlines pass.

## If a department has no current public vacancy page

Do not convert absence of a listing into proof that a fellowship does not exist, and do not send repeated informal messages asking for a place. First search the institution’s own domain and careers/news archive. If there is still no current notice, send one concise professional enquiry to the official department or education contact: identify your MS/DNB status, state the subspecialty route you are asking about, attach no unnecessary documents, and ask whether a current or upcoming application notice exists.

Record the reply as a lead, not as a final offer. A verbal assurance about “the next intake” does not settle eligibility, employment terms or a deadline. Wait for the written application process and preserve it with the rest of your evidence. This approach is slower than copying a directory, but it prevents a common career mistake: treating an old programme page as a live vacancy.

## How to compare two apparently similar fellowships

Two retina or cornea posts can have radically different training value for the same candidate. Use a question grid rather than a reputation-only comparison.

| Ask the programme | Why it changes the decision |
|---|---|
| What proportion is clinic, theatre, emergency, imaging, research and administration? | Reveals the actual weekly job |
| What is observed, assisted, performed under direct supervision and performed with graded autonomy? | Separates exposure from a training pathway |
| Who supervises, and how is feedback recorded? | Tests whether “mentorship” has a visible structure |
| What case discussions, journal clubs, audit or teaching occur? | Tests academic structure rather than brochure language |
| What is the rota, leave position and on-call responsibility? | Makes the workload visible before joining |
| What are the written employment terms: duration, stipend, accommodation, indemnity and bond? | Prevents assumptions about material terms |
| Can a current fellow explain a normal week? | Adds lived context, but does not override the written offer |

Avoid asking “Will I get to do surgery?” as a binary question. Ask which operations, at what stage, under what supervision, and how competence is assessed. The answer can be perfectly reasonable yet different from the work you want.

## Prepare the application package before a vacancy appears

Most institution-led applications need variations of the same evidence. Keep one controlled folder with versioned files:

- degree and registration documents requested by the employer;
- a concise CV with clear dates and no inflated case numbers;
- a logbook summary that separates observed, assisted and primary-surgeon work;
- abstracts, audit, presentations or publications if relevant, accurately cited;
- two referees who have agreed to be contacted; and
- a short cover note that says why the service’s current route fits your stated goal.

Do not use a generic “I am passionate about ophthalmology” cover note for every subspecialty. A retina candidate can say they want to build a structured approach to imaging, clinic decisions and supervised operative progression. A cornea candidate may instead explain the link between surface disease, graft work and the service they have verified. Be specific without pretending prior expertise you do not have.

## Interview: make your limits visible safely

Many programmes use a CV screen and a clinical discussion rather than one national fellowship examination. Rehearse a compact case presentation: problem representation, relevant examination and imaging, differential, what you did under supervision, what you would escalate, and what you learned. Never claim an independent procedure that you merely observed.

If the route also requires formal exam revision, the [fellowship preparation guide](/exams/fellowship) helps separate career selection from exam preparation. For practical communication rehearsal, inspect the [OSCE, Practical & Viva Voce Ready route](/osce). These internal pages are study resources, not an application authority; the institution’s written notice governs its process.

## The final 24-hour verification rule

Immediately before you apply, open the official notice again and check: closing time and time zone; eligibility; required registration; document format; application address or portal; fee if any; and whether the post is still accepting applications. Keep the page/PDF with the application copy. If any material point is unclear, ask the recruiting contact in writing rather than relying on a coaching group or an old spreadsheet.

That last check is the useful meaning of a “complete list”: complete enough for your actual decision because each short-listed post has a current source and an evidence trail.
