# How to Apply for FAICO: Documents, Deadlines and Common Rejections

FAICO eligibility is decided against the current All India Ophthalmological Society (AIOS) rules, not against an old coaching checklist. On the AIOS FAICO page checked on 18 August 2026, a candidate must be an AIOS member, hold a Government of India-recognised postgraduate ophthalmology qualification (or a recognised international qualification), and meet the stated post-PG experience plus subspecialty-training or practical-work route. The same page gives a 30 September 2026 cut-off for the experience requirements for that written-examination year.

The practical task is straightforward but unforgiving: turn each rule into one document that proves it, then compare every name, date, speciality and upload with the form before payment. The official AIOS notice, member portal and application form are the only places to rely on for the live cycle. They can change after this article is published.

> **2026 rule check:** AIOS currently lists 31 May 2026 as the last date of receipt, 30 August for the online FAICO MCQ test, and ₹10,000 per candidate per subject. These are time-sensitive operational facts, not dates to copy into a future application. Check the live [AIOS FAICO page](https://www.aios.org/article-66-about-faico.php) and your member portal before acting.

## Start with the eligibility decision, not the upload screen

The AIOS page currently sets out four layers. Read them in order.

| Requirement shown by AIOS | What it means for your file | Evidence to locate before the window opens |
|---|---|---|
| AIOS membership | You need an active member identity that the portal can recognise. | Membership details and the login route; resolve account-access problems before the deadline. |
| Basic postgraduate ophthalmology qualification | Your underlying qualification must fit the wording on the current AIOS page. | Degree/certificate and any registration evidence requested in the live form. |
| At least two years’ experience after completing postgraduation | Count from the completion event recognised in your supporting record, not from an assumed joining date. | Dated experience, fellowship or senior-residency certificates that make the start and end dates legible. |
| A subspecialty route | AIOS currently lists completion of a subspecialty fellowship or at least 18 months of fellowship, **or** completion of senior residency or at least 18 months of senior residency, **or** three years’ practical work in the subspecialty. | The certificate set that proves the one route you are using, with specialty and duration stated clearly. |

This table is a working translation of the live page, not an alternative regulation. A qualification that looks similar on a CV may not be enough if its certificate does not establish the fact AIOS needs to verify. If your route relies on a recognised international qualification, an interrupted fellowship, overlapping employment, an unusual institution name or a date that is close to the cut-off, obtain written clarification from AIOS. Do not let a friend’s successful route decide yours.

AIOS also says that a member may apply for only one speciality at a time and that one candidate can do a maximum of two specialities. Treat that as an application choice, not a minor field you can revise later. Decide the specialty before collecting letters so that the title, unit and work described in your evidence all point to the same application.

## Build a document-to-claim matrix

Most application trouble is not a lack of clinical experience. It is a weak link between a claim in the form and the document meant to prove it. Use a simple matrix before you open the portal.

| Claim you will make | Best supporting record | Pre-submit check |
|---|---|---|
| “I am the applicant named on this form.” | Government identity material if requested, profile details and photograph. | Spelling, initials, order of names and date of birth match wherever they appear. |
| “I hold the stated ophthalmology qualification.” | Degree or final certificate, and registration record where requested. | Qualification title is readable and matches the form exactly; do not abbreviate differently in different fields. |
| “I have the required post-PG experience.” | Employer/fellowship/senior-residency certificate on appropriate letterhead. | It states the role, specialty, start date, end date or continuing status, signatory and date of issue. |
| “I meet this subspecialty route.” | Specialty-specific fellowship, senior-residency or practice-experience evidence. | The stated specialty matches the FAICO subject selected; duration can be calculated from the record. |
| “I paid and submitted for this cycle.” | Portal acknowledgement and payment reference. | Save the confirmation immediately; do not rely only on a bank notification. |

Create a read-only PDF folder and use plain filenames: `01-qualification.pdf`, `02-membership.pdf`, `03-glaucoma-fellowship-certificate.pdf`, for example. That is not an AIOS requirement. It is a way to make final checking fast when you are balancing OPD, theatre and night duty. Keep original documents accessible too. A scan with a cropped signature, a missing second page or an unreadable seal can turn a clear fact into a question that takes time to resolve.

Do not manufacture an “experience certificate” by stitching together informal letters. Ask the institution to issue a dated record that can stand on its own. It should identify you, the post, the subspecialty, the period, and the authorised signatory. If an official certificate has wording you cannot change, do not alter it; ask AIOS what additional document, if any, it accepts.

## Put the current cycle on one sheet

The live AIOS page checked for this article states the following 2026 information. It is included to show how to plan, not to replace the live notice.

| Item | AIOS page checked 18 August 2026 | Your action |
|---|---|---|
| Experience cut-off | 30 September 2026 for requirements 3–6 | Calculate your route against that date and retain the calculation with your file. |
| Application deadline | 31 May 2026 | For any future cycle, substitute the newly published deadline and set an earlier personal deadline. |
| Online MCQ test | 30 August | Treat the portal/admit-card instructions as the final authority for timing and logistics. |
| Written-test centres listed | New Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Bangalore, Chennai and Hyderabad; the table also lists Madurai | Check the live notice and admit-card instructions for the authoritative centre allocation. |
| Fee | ₹10,000 per candidate per subject | Confirm current fee and payment method in the active portal before paying. |

Notice the centre discrepancy in the same page’s text and table. That is exactly why a candidate should not book travel from a secondary summary. Save the live announcement, then follow the final candidate instructions and admit card. Administrative facts move faster than study content.

## A submission workflow that survives a busy rota

### 1. Freeze the specialty choice

Before the portal opens, write the exact FAICO subject you are applying for at the top of your folder. Then check that the fellowship/senior-residency/practical-work certificate actually names that discipline. A general ophthalmology certificate may be valuable background but may not by itself establish a subspecialty claim.

### 2. Verify eligibility backwards from the cut-off

Make a one-line timeline: postgraduate completion; start and finish of fellowship or senior residency; any gap; 18-month or three-year threshold; applicable AIOS cut-off. Count calendar dates carefully. Where there is uncertainty, request written advice well before the closing date. Do not make a public page or an informal social-media answer carry more authority than AIOS’s written response.

### 3. Clean each evidence file

Open every PDF at 100%. Check page order, legibility, institutional letterhead, signatures, dates and full names. A colour scan can be useful if seals are faint, but file format, maximum size and upload requirements belong to the live portal. Never assume a previous year’s file-size rule still applies.

### 4. Enter the form from the documents, not from memory

Copy names, dates and qualification wording directly from the evidence. Memory creates harmless-looking differences: “Dr A. Kumar” in one field, “Anil Kumar” in another; “Glaucoma Fellowship” in one line and “Fellowship” in another. The issue is not that one version is necessarily wrong. It is that the reviewer cannot instantly connect them.

### 5. Save evidence of submission

After final submission and payment, save the acknowledgement, application number, payment reference and timestamp in a separate folder. Take a screenshot only as a supplement. The portal acknowledgement is more useful if later correspondence needs to refer to a record.

### 6. Watch for official communication

Use the email address and portal account associated with the application. Check spam filtering, but do not share credentials. If AIOS requests clarification, respond with the exact document asked for rather than sending an unstructured bundle. Keep the correspondence trail.

## “Common rejections” is the wrong phrase unless AIOS has said so

There is no public AIOS dataset on the page checked for this article that ranks reasons for refusal, and the Society’s page does not give a generic “rejection list.” It would be inaccurate to present anecdotal problems as official rules. The safer categories are **preventable file risks**:

- claiming a specialty that the supporting certificate does not name;
- leaving an experience interval impossible to calculate;
- using documents with inconsistent applicant names or dates;
- assuming an old deadline, fee or payment method remains active;
- uploading a partial, unreadable or wrongly ordered file; and
- treating a query for clarification as proof that an application has been rejected.

Each has the same solution: map each claim to one clean official record, use the live application instructions, and ask AIOS for written clarification where the rule is genuinely unclear. Do not “correct” a certificate yourself, infer a favourable interpretation, or wait until the final day to discover a portal or membership issue.

## Keep paperwork and FAICO preparation as separate tracks

Your application establishes eligibility; it does not prepare you for subject depth. Once the documents are controlled, build a clinical revision plan around the particular FAICO specialty. The site’s [FAICO preparation guide](/exams/faico) is the appropriate broad on-site route map. You can use [free FAICO sample questions](/free-mcqs/faico) for short retrieval blocks, and inspect [FAICO MCQs](/products/faico-mcqs) if its format fits your independent revision system. These are preparation resources, not AIOS material and not evidence of eligibility.

A useful working-week split is one administrative block until submission is confirmed, then protected subject blocks for reading, questions, imaging and oral explanation. Keep a two-column log: application actions on the left; clinical errors on the right. Mixing them creates the familiar result of repeatedly checking documents while neither revising nor resolving the actual uncertainty.

## Final 15-minute check before you submit

1. I have read the current AIOS page and the active portal instructions today.
2. My AIOS membership and chosen specialty are correct.
3. My postgraduate qualification matches the eligibility wording.
4. My single chosen experience route is demonstrable against the current cut-off.
5. Names, initials, dates and specialty wording agree across the form and files.
6. Each upload is complete, legible and in the portal’s required format.
7. I know the live deadline, fee and payment instruction, rather than a date copied from an old post.
8. I will save the acknowledgement and payment record after submission.
9. Any unresolved interpretation has been sent to AIOS in writing.

That is the whole objective: make it easy for a reviewer to verify the facts you are entitled to claim. For rules, dates, payment and venue, AIOS’s newest notice always wins over this guide.
