ophthamcq.org Indexation & organic-traffic audit 26 Aug 2026 158 URLs crawled

The site is not broken.
It is invisible.

A full crawl of all 158 published URLs found zero broken pages, zero canonical faults and zero duplicate titles. The technical build is sound. The reason nothing ranks is that the content went live eight days ago on a domain no other page on the web has ever linked to — while a second domain you already own holds the brand in Google and receives 701 one-way links from this one.

Assessment

Three causes, in order of weight: age, isolation, and a self-inflicted split between two domains.

Neither Google nor Bing returns a single result for site:ophthamcq.org. That is not a penalty and not a crawl block — Googlebot and Bingbot both receive clean 200s with fully rendered HTML. It is what a brand-new domain with no inbound links looks like in week two. Everything below is ordered by how much it is holding that back.

TIER I

Why nothing is indexed yet

These five explain the zero. Fix them and pages start entering the index within days to weeks — nothing below Tier I matters until they are addressed.

1
Root cause

The entire blog is eight days old

All 103 content drafts carry the same publication date and the last commit landed the day after. Ninety-nine of your 158 URLs — 63% of the site — have existed for just over a week. Google routinely takes two to eight weeks to crawl, render and index new pages on a domain it has no history with. There is no version of this where week-two traffic exists.

date: "2026-08-18" × 103 drafts  ·  last commit: 2026-08-19  ·  today: 2026-08-26
2
Root cause

No page anywhere on the web links to ophthamcq.org

A literal-string search for the domain returns nothing — not a directory, not a forum post, not a social profile. Google's primary discovery mechanism is links, and there are none to follow. A sitemap tells Google what exists; links tell it what is worth crawling. Without them, crawl budget on a new .org stays near the floor.

This is the single highest-leverage gap in the audit. One link from a domain Google already trusts will do more in a fortnight than any on-page change in this report.

3
Root cause

ophthamcq.com already owns the brand, and this site feeds it

ophthamcq.com is live on Vercel, indexed, and ranks first for the brand name. Every one of your 158 .org pages links out to it — 701 links in total, 24 on the homepage alone, every "Buy Now" and every product CTA. The .com links back zero times.

So the content site exports all of its authority to the storefront and receives none in return, while Google is left to work out which of two near-identical properties is the real OphthaMCQ. Today the answer is the .com, and the .org reads as a satellite pointing at it.

4
Amplifier

Ninety-nine posts published on a single day

Every draft shares one date, so from Google's side the site went from nothing to a 289,000-word archive overnight. On an established domain that is fine. On an eight-day-old one with no link history it looks like a content dump, and dumps get crawled cautiously and slowly rather than eagerly.

5
Amplifier

Nothing was ever actively pushed to an index

The IndexNow integration exists and the key file is live, but the build hook is gated behind INDEXNOW_SUBMIT=1 and Bing shows zero indexed URLs — strong evidence it has never actually run. Separately, not one of the 158 sitemap entries carries a <lastmod>, so crawlers get no freshness signal and no reason to prioritise anything.

TIER II

Why traffic would stay thin even after indexing

Assume Tier I is solved and all 158 pages enter the index tomorrow. These are the reasons the pages that matter most would still underperform.

6
Highest impact

The free-MCQ pages contain no indexable MCQs

src/data/free-mcqs.ts holds 193 real questions with options and explanations. Not one of them appears in the HTML Google receives. They are serialised into a 75 KB inline <script> and injected into the DOM by the quiz engine — and the engine renders one question at a time, so even a fully rendered page exposes a single question out of ten.

The result: seven pages targeting free ophthalmology MCQs, the highest-intent term the whole site is built around, average 350 words of boilerplate and a price list. This is the largest gap between content you own and content Google can see.

CTR

141 of 158 titles are too long to survive the SERP

Google truncates around 60–65 characters. Your product titles run to 118, stacking every exam name plus the brand into one string. When a title overflows Google either cuts it mid-phrase or rewrites it entirely, and you lose control of the one line that decides whether anyone clicks.

10
Site architecture

The blog posts barely link to each other

Across 103 drafts there are 14 blog-to-blog links in total, reaching 14 distinct posts. Eighty-five posts have no inbound link from any sibling article. The 444 internal links that do exist almost all point at hub pages — /osce, /free-mcqs, /glossary.

Nothing is orphaned, since /blog/ lists all 99. But there are no topical clusters, so Google sees 99 unrelated documents rather than a body of authority on FRCOphth, on DNB, on OSCE. Clustering is how a new site earns topical trust without backlinks — and it is the one Tier II fix that partially compensates for finding 2.

11
Policy risk

Self-serving review markup on the homepage

The homepage ships a Product node for OphthaMCQ itself carrying aggregateRating 4.8 with reviewCount 2400 and five hand-written five-star reviews. Google's structured-data policy makes reviews about the entity that controls the page ineligible for rich results, and unverifiable aggregate counts on your own brand are exactly the pattern that draws a manual action.

On a domain with no track record, a manual action would be far more costly than the star rating is worth. Move the testimonials to visible page content and drop the aggregateRating — or attach Product schema to the individual product pages, where genuine purchase reviews legitimately belong.

12
Distribution

Every social share renders blank

All 158 pages declare og:image = /og-image.jpg. That file returns 404. Every WhatsApp forward, every Telegram drop into a residents' group, every LinkedIn post shows an empty card — on a site whose audience shares study material through exactly those channels. Given finding 2, this is also the channel most likely to produce your first real inbound links.

TIER III

Technical hygiene

Real defects, none of them the reason for the zero. Worth clearing in a single pass.

13
Duplicate origin

http:// serves 200 instead of redirecting, and there is no HSTS

http://ophthamcq.org/blog/ returns a full 200 OK rather than a 301 to HTTPS, and no Strict-Transport-Security header is set. That is a complete second copy of all 158 pages on an insecure origin. The www → apex redirect and trailing-slash normalisation both work correctly, so this is the one redirect rule missing.

14
Robots

Cloudflare is injecting its own robots.txt above yours

The file served at /robots.txt is not the one in public/. Cloudflare's managed AI-crawler block is prepended to it, producing two conflicting User-agent: * groups and a Disallow: / followed later by Allow: / for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider, Amazonbot, Applebot-Extended and meta-externalagent.

Googlebot is unaffected — it is never disallowed and resolves conflicts in favour of Allow. But AI crawlers that honour the first matching group will read Disallow and leave. In 2026, with students routinely asking ChatGPT which question bank to use, that is a live channel closing itself. Decide the policy in one place: either turn off Cloudflare's managed robots.txt and keep yours, or delete yours and let Cloudflare's stand.

15
Layout stability

No image carries width or height

Zero of the 17 homepage images declare dimensions, so the browser cannot reserve space and every one contributes to layout shift. Product images are also hot-linked from appx-content-v2.classx.co.in as raw PNGs with no srcset and no WebP — a third-party dependency in your largest contentful paint.

16
Housekeeping

Duplicate sitemaps and short product descriptions

/sitemap.xml and /sitemap-0.xml are byte-identical (14,566 bytes each) — harmless, but submit only one. Separately, product meta descriptions are marketing taglines rather than search copy: "Build Unshakeable Foundations. Revise Anatomy the Exam Way." is 44 characters and contains none of the terms the page targets.

17
Security · not SEO

A live API key is committed in the repository

src/lib/thestacc.ts hardcodes a production key as a fallback, with a source comment acknowledging it. It is in the repo and in git history. Unrelated to indexing, but it should be rotated before anything else in this report ships.

PLAN

What to do, in order

Sequenced by what actually unblocks indexing rather than by effort. Week 1 is almost entirely distribution and plumbing; it matters more than every code change beneath it.

Week 1 · get discovered

Target: first URLs appearing in Google's index within 10–14 days.

  1. Link ophthamcq.com → ophthamcq.org.The single highest-leverage action available. Put a real navigation link on the .com — header, footer, and a "Study guides" entry. It costs nothing and it is the only trusted link you can grant yourself.
  2. Submit the sitemap in Search Console and request indexing on 20 priority URLs.Homepage, the six exam hubs, the four compare pages, and your nine strongest posts. Verification is already in DNS, so this is minutes of work.
  3. Add <lastmod> to the sitemap.Drive it from file mtime or frontmatter last_updated. Without it crawlers have no freshness signal on any of 158 URLs.
  4. Run IndexNow for real.Set INDEXNOW_SUBMIT=1 in the deploy environment and submit all 158 URLs once. Bing indexes far faster than Google on new domains and will confirm the pipeline works.
  5. Ship an og-image and force HTTPS.Fixes findings 12 and 13 together — one asset, one redirect rule, plus an HSTS header.
  6. Post the best five articles where the audience already is.Ophthalmology residency subreddits, FRCOphth and DNB Telegram and WhatsApp groups, AIOS forums, LinkedIn. This is how you manufacture the first links Google needs.
Week 2 · make the pages worth ranking

Target: the commercial and high-intent pages can compete once crawled.

  1. Server-render the MCQs.Emit all ten questions, options and explanations into static HTML, then let the quiz engine progressively enhance them. Turns seven 350-word shells into seven substantial pages on your best keyword.
  2. Rewrite all 141 over-length titles to 55–60 characters.One primary keyword plus the brand. Drop the exam-name pile-up — that belongs in H2s and body copy.
  3. Add Product schema to all 21 product pages.Name, image, description, offers with the ₹ price, availability and currency. The prices are already on the page; only the markup is missing.
  4. Add BreadcrumbList site-wide and a WebSite node on the homepage.Both belong in Layout.astro, so it is one edit for all 158 pages.
  5. Remove the homepage aggregateRating.Keep the testimonials as visible content. Reattach review markup to individual products later, once you have verifiable purchase reviews.
  6. Rewrite the 21 short meta descriptions.140–155 characters, leading with the term the page targets.
Weeks 3–4 · build topical authority

Target: clustered topics Google can recognise as expertise, not 99 loose documents.

  1. Build internal link clusters.Group the 99 posts by exam — FRCOphth, DNB, ICO/FICO, FAICO, PDCET, OSCE — give each a hub page, and link every post to its hub and to three or four siblings. Target 8–12 internal links per post, up from 4.3.
  2. Expand the four compare pages to 1,200–1,800 words.Real feature tables, real pricing, honest strengths of the alternative. These are your highest-converting queries and currently your thinnest pages.
  3. Resolve the two-domain split deliberately.Either consolidate onto one domain, or keep both with the .com as storefront and the .org as the content property — and in that case give the .org its own footer identity and reciprocal links so it stops reading as a satellite.
  4. Settle the robots.txt conflict.Pick Cloudflare's managed file or your own, not both, and make the AI-crawler policy consistent in whichever survives.
  5. Add width and height to every image; self-host the product images as WebP.Removes both the layout shift and the third-party dependency in LCP.
Ongoing

Target: compounding, and honest expectations about the curve.

  1. Rotate the leaked API key.Before any of the above ships.
  2. Publish on a cadence, not in batches.Two to four posts a week from the remaining 164 planned ideas beats another hundred-post drop.
  3. Watch Search Console, not Analytics.Coverage and Impressions move weeks before Clicks does. Impressions rising with clicks flat is the expected shape at month one — not a failure.

Realistic timeline: first indexed pages in 10–20 days once week 1 ships, first meaningful impressions at 6–10 weeks, first material organic traffic at 3–5 months. That curve is the normal cost of a new domain — the content to fill it is already written and already good.
